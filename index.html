More than a view

A living, breathing landscape.

Yellowstone sits atop a vast volcanic system, giving rise to geysers, hot springs, mud pots, and fumaroles. Beyond the thermal basins, forests, rivers, lakes, and open valleys support a remarkable range of life.

Give the park time. Distances are longer than they look on a map, wildlife moves on its own schedule, and road conditions can change quickly.

A three-day starting point

  1. 01

    Geyser basins

    Explore the Old Faithful area and nearby boardwalks. Start early and allow time for parking and predicted eruptions.

  2. 02

    Canyon & waterfalls

    Take in the canyon viewpoints and waterfalls. Choose a few overlooks rather than trying to see every stop.

  3. 03

    Lake & wildlife watching

    Enjoy Yellowstone Lake, then scan open valleys from a safe, designated pullout—bring binoculars and patience.

Respect wildlife · Stay on trails · Leave no trace

Keep your distance. Keep it wild.

Wildlife belongs here. We’re visitors.

Seeing animals in their natural habitat is a privilege. Use binoculars or a zoom lens, never approach or feed wildlife, and follow park distance rules.

Keep your distance

Grizzly bears

Give bears plenty of space and carry bear spray where recommended. Never approach wildlife for a photo.

Icon of the plains

American bison

Bison are powerful, unpredictable wild animals. Stay well back and never block their path.

Look from afar

Wolves

Wolves are elusive and often seen at a distance. A spotting scope can turn a faraway glimpse into a memorable one.

Before you go

Good trips start with good information.

Yellowstone is a changing, seasonal place. Official park updates are the best source for current access and safety information.

⌁

Check conditions

Roads, trails, services, and weather can change. Check current conditions on the official NPS site before traveling.

◷

Allow extra time

Expect slow drives, construction, traffic, and wildlife jams. Keep a safe following distance and never stop in the road.

♧

Protect the park

Stay on boardwalks around thermal features, pack out litter, and leave natural and cultural objects where they are.
